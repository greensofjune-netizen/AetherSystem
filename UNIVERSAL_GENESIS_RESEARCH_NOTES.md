## Source note

### The Universal Genesis Telemetry: 13.8 Billion Years of System Scaling

To compile the definitive research and development file on how everything
began, the universe can be treated as a self-assembling physical system.
Cosmological observations provide a sequence from the hot early universe to the
present day.

#### Phase 1: The Initial Instantiation

The early universe underwent rapid expansion. Inflation is a leading theoretical
proposal, not a directly observed event, for explaining flatness, horizon
uniformity, and the origin of large-scale density perturbations. As the universe
cooled, quarks and gluons became hadrons, and a small matter surplus over
antimatter remained. The mechanism of baryogenesis is not yet established.

- **Inflationary epoch proposal:** approximately \(10^{-36}\) to \(10^{-32}\)
  seconds, in common inflationary models. The exact timing and degree of
  expansion are model-dependent.
- **Quark and hadron epochs:** approximately \(10^{-12}\) to 1 second.
- **Baryogenesis:** the excess of matter over antimatter is observed; its exact
  mechanism remains unresolved.

#### Phase 2: Elemental Baseline

- **Big Bang nucleosynthesis:** roughly 3 to 20 minutes after the Big Bang.
  Standard nucleosynthesis predicts primordial matter dominated by about 75%
  hydrogen and 25% helium by mass, with much smaller quantities of deuterium,
  helium-3, and lithium.
- **Recombination and photon decoupling:** approximately 380,000 years after
  the Big Bang, at a temperature near 3,000 K. Neutral atoms formed and photons
  began to travel freely. We observe those photons today as the cosmic
  microwave background.

#### Phase 3: First Stars and Cosmic Structure

After recombination, the universe entered an era often called the cosmic dark
ages because there were no luminous stars or galaxies yet. The first stars,
commonly called Population III, are estimated to have formed roughly a few
hundred million years after the Big Bang, with timing dependent on model and
observation.

Those early stars and later galaxy formation contributed ultraviolet radiation
that reionized intergalactic hydrogen. Reionization developed over cosmic time
and is generally understood to have completed around one billion years after
the Big Bang, not nine billion years after.

Stellar nucleosynthesis and stellar explosions created most naturally occurring
elements heavier than primordial hydrogen and helium. Contributions also come
from neutron-star mergers and other processes. It is therefore more accurate to
say that much of the material in bodies, planets, and tools has a stellar and
cosmic provenance than to say that every heavy atom followed one supernova
pathway.

#### Phase 4: Solar System and Stewardship

- **Solar system formation:** approximately 4.6 billion years ago from a
  collapsing molecular cloud and protoplanetary disk.
- **Planetary accretion:** dust and gas aggregated into planetesimals, planets,
  moons, and smaller bodies through gravitational, collisional, and chemical
  processes.
- **Life and observation:** the exact origin of life is unresolved. Biological
  evolution later produced organisms capable of reasoning, language, tool use,
  and scientific observation.
