WHAT YOU ARE SEEING

Not a wave turning into a little ball.

01

Localized detection

Light is quantum. Every photon produces a localized detection, even when interference is present. The dots are measurement outcomes, not pictures of photons flying along a path.

02

Wave-like statistics

Indistinguishable paths contribute amplitudes that interfere. As many photons arrive, alternating bright and dark fringes emerge. No individual detection needs to trace a wave.

03

Information changes the pattern

Entanglement with a path marker makes paths distinguishable. This ideal balanced-path model uses V = sqrt(1 - D squared). At D = 1, interference disappears, but single-slit diffraction remains.

Physics model, entropy definitions & limitations

We use the small-angle, far-field double-slit approximation: I(x) is proportional to sinc squared(pi a x / (lambda L)) times [1 + V cos(2 pi d x / (lambda L))], where sinc(z) = sin(z)/z. The 120 bin-center weights are normalized over this finite screen, then sampled. Sub-bin x positions are uniform. The schematic is not to scale.

D represents ideal which-path distinguishability for balanced slits with pure path-marker states, giving V = sqrt(1 - D squared). The reduced path state's eigenvalues are (1 + V)/2 and (1 - V)/2; their von Neumann entropy ranges from 0 to 1 bit. This simplified relation does not cover every noisy or unbalanced experiment.

Screen Shannon entropy H = -sum p log2 p is a different quantity, dependent on bin size and screen extent. Rolling entropy estimates it from up to 128 samples. Surprisal -log2 P(bin) describes an individual outcome. The 8-bit rare-bin threshold is a visualization convention, not a physical threshold or proof of collapse. None of these charts establishes a wave-to-particle transition.

Randomness comes from JavaScript Math.random. This is an educational simulation, not a quantum random-number generator, detector, hardware experiment, or cryptographic entropy source. No Aether runtime, network connection, accounts, or external libraries are used. Reloading closes the in-memory run; export the histogram if you want to keep it.

FROM YOUR NOTEBOOK

Equilibrium equation

Dimensionless equilibrium sandbox

Chosen assumptions: a one-dimensional domain Ω = [0, 1] with abstract coordinate u, not detector position; W(u) = w·u, P(u) = p, ξ(u) = ξ, and Seco(u) = s. All variables, derivatives, and E are dimensionless (unit 1). The fields are time-independent; W(0) = 0 and W(1) = w specify the boundary flux. These are modeling choices, not measurements.

Choose the notebook fields

ξ = 1 weights only the wave term; ξ = 0 weights only the particle term, following your clarification. This is separate from the experiment's which-path slider D.

In one dimension ∇·(Wξ) = d(w·u·ξ)/du = wξ, so the exact integral is E = s[wξ − p(1−ξ)]. When s > 0 and w + p > 0, E = 0 requires ξ = p/(w+p). No numerical quadrature is needed.

This is an algebraic residual, not energy, a conservation law, or a stability test. The sandbox does not change photon detections, the cohesion proxy, or CSV exports. Its settings reset on reload.
